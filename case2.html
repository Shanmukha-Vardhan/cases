THE ARAKU VISTADOME ENIGMA // THE GHOST IN TUNNEL 22

ROUTE: VIZAG ➔ ARAKU VALLEY
SCENE: COACH EV-1 (LOCKED TRAIN)

LEAD INVESTIGATOR BRIEFING: On Sunday morning, Train #18551 (Vizag to Araku Kirandul Express) entered Tunnel 22 between Tyda and Borra Caves in the Eastern Ghats. The tunnel is 2,750 meters long. The train was plunged into total darkness for exactly 3 minutes and 40 seconds (8:42:00 AM to 8:45:40 AM) due to a coach generator failure. Both vestibule exit doors were locked from the inside by TTE Surya Prakash. When the train exited the tunnel into daylight over the Ananthagiri Gorge bridge, billionaire coffee tycoon Bhadri Raju (Seat 14) was found stabbed to death with an antique Araku tribal dagger. His briefcase was unlocked and the fabled Kalinga Emerald was gone.

🔎 THE 5 CRITICAL TRAIN CLUES (CLICK TO INSPECT)
CLUE 1: The Tribal Dagger & Angle of Entry
The dagger entered upward at 45 degrees into the 5th left intercostal space. Bhadri's seat was swiveled facing the right window. A standing assailant from the aisle could NOT have made that thrust without standing directly in the tight 14-inch gap between Seat 14 and the window wall!
CLUE 2: The Luminescent Cave Moss Traces
Forensic scraping of the floorboard by Seat 14 found *Dictyostelium viride*, a rare glowing moss that grows ONLY on the wet granite walls of Tunnel 22 and Borra Caves vents. Who had this damp moss on their clothing?
CLUE 3: Dr. Lavanya's Secret Voice Recording
Dr. Lavanya claimed her headphones were on. But her phone was secretly recording ambient sound! Audio analysis reveals soft footsteps counting in Telugu ("Okaటి, rendu, moodu...")—someone memorized the exact number of strides in the dark from the rear lounge to Seat 14!
CLUE 4: The Water Dispenser Ice Flask
Where was the Kalinga Emerald hidden? Railway police searched every bag. Nothing. But when inspecting the lounge water dispenser, inside the ice reservoir, a sealed stainless steel thermos was submerged—housing the 120-carat Kalinga Emerald!
CLUE 5: The Tripped Circuit Breaker
The electrical panel in the rear cubicle was inspected. The lighting fuse was not blown by a surge—it had been intentionally wedged open with an ordinary plastic coffee stirrer!
